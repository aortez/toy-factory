## Prospective population and renewal criteria

Observe `(384,608]`: 192 days of recruitment, then 32 days of follow-up for the
last eligible births. The primary cohort is born in `(384,576]`, divided into
three windows `(384,448]`, `(448,512]` and `(512,576]`. Each window contains two
scheduled disturbance opportunities. The windows are deliberately 64 rather
than 32 days; this is a changed prospective temporal criterion and must not be
applied retrospectively to the earlier experiment.

A garden passes renewal if each birth window contains at least one plant that
survives 32 days, and there is at least one direct parent–child pair with both
members born in the primary cohort and both surviving 32 days. Count exact
lifetimes, not generation labels or purchases. Death at or before the age-32
deadline fails establishment; later death does not undo it. Allow posthumous
children, but do not grant a dead parent an age it never reached. Report
established chain depths, all primary survival/death statuses and later births
separately; births after day 576 cannot enter the primary denominator.

Population viability uses the existing thresholds: no empty interval, mean
living count at least four in every 16-day block, endpoint at least four. A
broad within-panel renewal signal requires all six recurring worlds viable,
at least four passing renewal, coverage of all three weather seeds and both
layouts, and more renewal passes than stopped. This is three paired weather
histories, not six independent weather samples.

Report imposed and natural deaths, including deaths at or before age 32, all
population minima/means, seed purchases/fates, species and founder-family
representation. Use exact continuous absence intervals as well as 16-day
checkpoints; distinguish living absence from losing both plants and pending
seeds. Flag baseline groups retained continuously by stopped but not recurring
as diversity tradeoffs. All losses remain visible, including pre-day-384
monocultures. Individual deaths and family turnover are not automatic renewal
vetoes, but a renewal pass alone does not establish acceptable ecological costs
or qualify the environment for broad training.

- Six day-384 reference calls on the frozen earlier executable.
- Twelve day-384 calls on the new executable, both arms.
- Twelve day-512 intermediate calls, both arms.
- Twelve day-608 endpoints, both arms.

- Twelve day-608 calls without framebuffer capture.

Short contract fixtures and native unit tests are separate from panel research.
Freeze protocol, actual analysis/build sources, current and reference binaries,
model and build cache before the first call. Runtime changes are confined to
the host CLI and fixed patch helper/header; no ecology or controller changes.
Use a sanitized build with the same settings and fresh output paths.

Every new anchor must equal its frozen-executable reference in state, complete
ledgers, old event receipts and pixels, after removing only the new declared
mode metadata/kind/hash wrapper. Validate all anchors before longer calls.
Intermediate and endpoint ledgers must reproduce the anchor prefix; endpoints
must also reproduce their own day-512 prefix and earlier event receipts. Audit
every event's complete spatial victim set, age, stores, death classification
and before/after state hashes. Reject unreceipted deaths or omitted victims.
Check exact population conservation and fixed storage bounds. Repeat state,
ledgers and pixels must match; capture-free results may differ only in the
absent framebuffer CRC.

Retain all 24 paired day-512/day-608 images, complete seed/lifetime ledgers,
source identities and call receipts. Keep earlier scores closed. Do not create
per-step traces or copy older full bundles. Reauthenticate inputs at completion
and verify the portable capture and local bundle without executing a simulator.
Stop on any mismatch; preserve failures without automatic retry, changed seeds,
replacement worlds, window changes or schedule tuning. Publish all paired
outcomes, costs and a recommendation before proposing another experiment.
